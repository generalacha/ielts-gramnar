Cohesion

Linking adjuncts and linking & sequencing events.

Linking adjuncts

Linking adjuncts show the connection between one sentence or clause and another. The two main ideas here are alternatives and contrasts.

as opposed to + noun / -ing
He relaxed at home, as opposed to going out.
instead of + noun / -ing
She stayed home instead of going out.
although / though + clause
Although it was raining, we went out.
however / nevertheless
It was raining. However, we went out.

Important: however can appear at the beginning or the end of a sentence. As a sentence-linking adjunct, though normally appears later in the sentence, not as “Though, ...” after a full stop.

Linking and sequencing events

These words and phrases put events in order. The key choices are when, after, after that, afterwards, next, subsequently, later on, followed by, go on to and finally.

Start / timewhen / after
Next eventafter that / next
Later eventsubsequently / later on
Endfinally / in the end
when / after + clause
After he heard the news, he went home.
after + -ing
After hearing the news, he went home.
after that / afterwards
We saw a film and after that we had dinner.
go on to + infinitive
She went on to become a manager.

Remember: subsequently is more formal than next and is common in written English. After that is useful for a list of events; afterwards refers back to the event just mentioned. After is not normally used by itself as an adverb.
