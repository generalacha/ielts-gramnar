IELTS Grammar

Functions and notions

Appearances, cause and result, behaviour, likelihood, surprise, similarity and opinion.

Appearances

Use these patterns to describe how something appears, sounds, behaves or seems.

PatternFormal / neutralInformal / spoken
verb + comparisonas if / as thoughlike
appear / seemappears to be / seems to havelooks like / seems like
look / sound / act / behave / smell + as if / as though
He looked as though he had been drinking.
Informal: like
It seems like it's going to rain.
As if and as though have the same meaning. Like is especially common in informal English.

Characteristic behaviour

These forms describe behaviour that is habitual, typical or repeatedly true.

MeaningUseful formExample
usual behaviourusually / always + simple tenseHe always has lunch in a restaurant.
past habitused to + infinitiveThey used to eat meat.
repeated / annoyingkeep + -ingHe keeps telling me what to do.
typical tendencytend to + infinitiveI tend to use my phone for messages.
habitual patternbe in the habit of + -ingWe're not in the habit of spending much.
Criticism: always / forever + present continuous can criticise repeated behaviour: “She's forever complaining.”

Similarity

Like and as both express similarity, but they normally take different structures.

FormRuleExample
like+ noun / pronounShe sings like a bird.
as+ subject + verbNobody sings as I do.
as ... asequal degreeHer film is as good as the last one.
as much ... asuncountable quantityHe earns as much money as a director.
as many ... ascountable quantityThere weren't as many people as expected.
