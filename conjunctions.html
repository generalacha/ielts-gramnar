Because & so

Because introduces a reason. So introduces a result.

Reason
She bought the dress because she liked it.
Result
She liked the dress, so she bought it.
IELTS: in more formal writing, you can often replace spoken so with as a result or therefore when linking separate sentences.

But, however; although, even though; in spite of, despite

All of these express contrast, but the grammar after them is different.

FormPatternExample
butclause + but + clauseHe is intelligent, but he cannot get a job.
howeversentence. However, sentence.He is intelligent. However, he cannot get a job.
although / even though+ clauseEven though he is intelligent, he cannot get a job.
in spite of / despite+ noun / -ingDespite being intelligent, he cannot get a job.
Formal / neutral: even though · in spite of · despite · however   |   Informal / spoken: but · though

If, when & unless

If, when and unless + present simple can refer to future time.

If
Perhaps it will happen.
The dog will not bite if you are gentle.
When
It is expected to happen.
The dog will not bite when it is asleep.
Unless
= if ... not
The dog will not bite unless you frighten it.
Important: after if / when / unless, use the present simple for this future meaning, not will.

If & in case

They are not the same. If gives a condition. In case explains a precaution taken before a possible event.

If = condition
If the baby wakes up, feed her.
The action happens after the condition.
In case = precaution
In case the baby wakes up, I have left some milk.
The preparation happens before the possible event.
Informal spoken: just in case is very common. Example: “Take an umbrella just in case.”

As long as

As long as means only if / on condition that.

RegisterExpressionExample
formalon condition thatYou may use the room on condition that you leave it tidy.
neutral / spokenas long asYou can use my mobile as long as you do not speak for too long.
Meaning: permission or result is possible only when the condition is satisfied.
