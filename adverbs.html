IELTS Grammar

Adverbs

Degree, frequency, manner, qualifying adverbs and sequencers.

Degree

Adverbs of degree show how much or how strongly something is true.

1 slight→2 moderate→3 quite strong→4 strong→5 extreme

Frequency

Adverbs of frequency show how often something happens.

0%→10%→40%→60%→80%→100%

Manner

Adverbs of manner describe how an action happens. They are used with verbs and commonly appear in the middle or at the end of a sentence.

Most adjectives
slow → slowly · patient → patiently · proud → proudly
-le → -ly
gentle → gently
consonant + y → -ily
angry → angrily
one-syllable -y → + ly
shy → shyly
-ic → -ically
automatic → automatically
Do not split verb + object
✗ He opened slowly the door.
✓ He opened the door slowly.
MeaningFormal / neutralInformal / spoken

easilywith relative easepretty easily

Qualifying adverbs

Qualifying adverbs make statements less absolute and less assertive. This is especially useful in IELTS Writing when you want to sound careful rather than overgeneralise.

IdeaFormal / neutralInformal / spoken
almost / nearlyto a large extent / virtuallypretty much
relative degreeto some extent / relativelykind of
probabilityin all probability / probablymost likely
generalisationin general / generallymost of the time
IELTS idea: instead of an absolute statement, qualify it. Technology is harmful → Technology can be harmful in some cases.

Sequencers

Sequencers join ideas and show the order in which things happen.

BeginningFirst
First, we went to the supermarket.
Middlethen · after that · next
Then we went to the clothes shop.
Endfinally
Finally, we went home.
Next, after that and then can be used in any order in the middle of a sequence.
StageFormal / neutralInformal / spoken
beginningto begin withfirst of all
middlefollowing thisafter that
middlesubsequentlyand then
endin conclusionin the end
