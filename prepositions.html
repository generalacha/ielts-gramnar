IELTS Grammar

Prepositions

Core uses, time expressions, dependent prepositions and common contrasts.

What are they?

Prepositions express a relationship between one person or thing and another.

PrepositionExample
toI speak to John.
inI live in Barcelona.
fromThe email is from my brother.
forI have a present for you.

During & for

Both refer to time, but they answer different questions.

during + period/eventAnswers When?
We spoke English during lunch breaks.
for + quantity of timeAnswers How long?
They worked there for many years.
Memory: during = inside an event/period · for = duration.

Dependent prepositions

Many nouns, verbs and adjectives are followed by a fixed preposition. Learn them as one expression.

ExpressionExample
have access toStudents need access to technology.
take responsibility forYou must take responsibility for your learning.
be good atShe is good at organising her time.
believe inI believe in learning by practice.
choose fromThere are many options to choose from.
concentrate onConcentrate on your pronunciation.
depend onIt depends on the situation.
insist onThey insist on arriving early.
prevent ... fromThis prevents people from making mistakes.
struggle withHe struggles with verb endings.
laugh at → a funny person/thingThey laughed at the joke.
laugh about → a funny situationLater, we laughed about what happened.
think of → produce/remember an ideaCan you think of another example?
think about → consider a situationI need to think about the problem.

Like

Like has three very common uses in this lesson.

Ask for a descriptionWhat is she like?
Similar toShe looks like her mother.
For exampleI do things like cooking and washing up.

For & since

Use both to say how long, especially with the present perfect when an action started in the past and continues now.

for + periodI've worked here for three years.
since + starting pointI've worked here since 2023.
Present perfect: I've lived here for three years. · I've lived here since last January.

Prepositions in questions

In normal spoken English, the preposition is often placed at the end of the question.

Natural / spokenWhat can I write this letter with?
Where do you come from?
What is it used for?
More formalWith what can I write this letter?
From where do you come?
For IELTS Speaking, the version with the preposition at the end is completely natural.
