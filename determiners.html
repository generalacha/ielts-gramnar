Determiners — summary

Determiners help us identify nouns or describe quantity. These three lessons focus on quantity expressions, some / any, and when to use the with geographical names.

TopicKey formsMain rule
quantitya few · a little · a lot of · much · manyChoose according to countable / uncountable nouns.
some / anysome · anyBoth work with plural countables and uncountables, but the meaning and sentence type differ.
placestheUse the with certain geographical features and plural place names.

A few, a little, a lot of, much, many

The most important distinction is countable plural vs uncountable.

UseUncountable nounPlural countable noun
small quantitya little coffeea few apples
large quantitya lot of coffeea lot of apples
negativenot much coffeenot many apples
questionmuch coffee?many apples?
a little
uncountable → a little time · a little money
a few
plural countable → a few people · a few questions
much / many
especially common in questions and negatives
a lot of
works with both countable and uncountable nouns
Quick memory: few / many → things you can count. little / much → things you do not count individually.

Some & any

Use both with uncountable nouns and plural countable nouns.

SituationUseExample
positive statementsomeWe've got some sugar.
request expecting “yes”someCan I have some water?
open questionanyHave you got any questions?
negative meaninganyWe never have any sugar.
it does not matter whichanyAny option is better than none.
Key idea: some usually refers to a restricted or expected quantity; any is more open or unrestricted.
